# Copywriting principles for small-business owners

Status: draft for owner approval. Nothing here is new policy. Each rule is
taken from `PRODUCT.md` or `DESIGN.md` in this repository and cites where it
comes from. If a source changes, this file must change with it.

## Who the copy is for

`PRODUCT.md` (Users): small business owners are the primary public-site
audience. They arrive wanting a useful starting template for work their
business needs to manage. Visitors may also arrive with a business goal, a
chosen template, a specific capability, or a request for help (Operating
Context).

## Principles

1. **Start with the business task, in plain words.**
   Explain the business outcome before internal product structure
   (`PRODUCT.md`, Product Principles). Plain descriptions of everyday business
   tasks lead the public journey, and a business task comes before technical
   details (`DESIGN.md`, Key Characteristics and Do's). The home page says what
   the software helps with, which is tracking sales and stock, before it
   points to templates.

2. **Be specific about what a template includes and what setup needs.**
   Show this before asking for signup (`PRODUCT.md`, Product Principles).
   Keep template contents visible (`DESIGN.md`, Do's).

3. **Say only what is verified.**
   Prices, testimonials, customer counts, integrations, implementation
   times, performance measures and customer outcomes need verified evidence
   (`PRODUCT.md`, Capabilities and Constraints). The project has no customer
   interviews, conversion data, approved testimonials or verified performance
   claims (Evidence on Hand), so none of these may appear in copy until
   evidence exists. Do not invent numbers.

4. **Never imply something is live before the customer approves it.**
   Do not imply a template or workspace is live before the customer approves
   its configuration (`PRODUCT.md`; `DESIGN.md`, Don'ts). A generated first
   version is reviewed by the customer before it is activated. Make review and
   activation control explicit in the words, not only in the interface.

5. **Use the agreed names.**
   Kigae is the customer-facing and open-source name. Senumi is the sponsor.
   Use Kigae Engine only when prose must separate the self-hosted runtime from
   Kigae Cloud. Keep legacy Kora spellings only in technical examples that
   need them for compatibility (`PRODUCT.md`, Brand Commitments).

6. **Keep the reading voice clear.**
   Mono-style metadata and small labels are for counts, categories and
   indices, not for the main message (`DESIGN.md`, Typography and Don'ts).
   Eyebrow and kicker labels are listed there as a known defect, not a rule,
   so do not add new ones.

7. **Tell people what happens when something goes wrong.**
   Setup must keep the visitor's choices and input through loading, network
   failure and retry (`PRODUCT.md`, Operating Context and Product Principles).
   Error and retry copy should not contradict that. Only say choices are
   kept where the product really keeps them.

## What these sources do not say

These points are not decided in `PRODUCT.md` or `DESIGN.md`, so this file
does not decide them either:

- Any claim about African markets or customers. `PRODUCT.md` says to validate
  business types, buying roles and local operating constraints with customers
  before making stronger segment claims.
- Currency (such as KES), payment methods (such as M-Pesa), languages or
  local idioms. Add these only after the owner confirms the product supports
  them and customers have been consulted.
- Tone words beyond "plain" and "direct". The sources set no further style.
